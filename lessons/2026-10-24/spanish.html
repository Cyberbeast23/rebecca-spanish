👋 Spanish Week 1: Greetings and introductions

¡Hola, Rebecca! Welcome to your very first Spanish lesson. This week is all about the first thirty seconds of any conversation: saying hello, the right greeting for the time of day, asking how someone is, telling them your name, and saying goodbye. By the end you'll be able to hold the little conversation in Part 2 all by yourself. The audio is Latin American Spanish, spoken a little slowly.

Five sounds to know first:
  • h is silent: hola sounds like OH-lah.
  • ll sounds like English y: me llamo is meh YAH-moh.
  • Vowels are short and pure: a = "ah", e = "eh", i = "ee", o = "oh", u = "oo". They never change.
  • Accent marks show the loud syllable: adiós is ah-dee-OHS, días is DEE-ahs.
  • Upside-down marks open a question or exclamation: ¿Cómo estás? and ¡Hola! You don't need to type them in the quizzes.

Grammar Tip: Friendly or polite?

Spanish has two ways to say "you". Use tú (friendly) with friends, family, and people your age. Use usted (polite) with older people, strangers, and at work.

Friendly (tú)Polite (usted)
How are you?¿Cómo estás?¿Cómo está usted?
What's your name?¿Cómo te llamas?¿Cómo se llama usted?
And you?¿Y tú?¿Y usted?

To answer "What's your name?", just say Me llamo + your name: Me llamo Rebecca. It literally means "I call myself Rebecca." Not sure which "you" to use? Start with usted; people will tell you if they'd like tú.

Culture Corner

🌎 In Mexico and most of Latin America, it's polite to greet everyone when you walk into a small shop, a waiting room, or an elevator, usually with a quick ¡Buenos días! or ¡Buenas tardes! Skipping the greeting can seem a little cold.

⏰ The switch from buenos días to buenas tardes happens around noon (or after lunch), and buenas noches is used once it's dark, for both "good evening" and "good night".

😘 Friends and new acquaintances often greet with one light kiss on the right cheek (two in Spain). A handshake is fine in business. And hasta luego ("see you later") is used even when you may never see the person again; it's just a warm way to say goodbye.
