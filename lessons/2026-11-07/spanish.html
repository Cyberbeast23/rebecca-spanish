👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Spanish Week 3: Family (la familia)

¡Hola otra vez, Rebecca! You can already say hello and count to ten. This week is all about la familia — the people closest to you. You'll learn how to name parents, siblings, kids, and grandparents, and how to introduce them. The conversation in Part 2 is Ana introducing her family to you. The audio is Latin American Spanish, spoken a little slowly.

Sounds to listen for this week:
  • h is silent: hijo and hija start with a vowel sound — EE-hoh, EE-hah.
  • Stress on the second-last syllable: most of these words are loud on the middle: fa-MI-lia, her-MA-no, a-BUE-lo.
  • ue sounds like "weh": abuelo and abuela have a quick "weh" in the middle.
  • d between vowels is soft: in padre and madre, the d is gentle, almost like the "th" in "the" (but don't force it).
  • Gender endings: many masculine words end in -o (hermano, hijo, abuelo) and feminine ones in -a (hermana, hija, abuela).

Grammar Tip: Spanish nouns have gender (el / la)

Every Spanish noun is either masculine or feminine. You show that with the little word in front:

Masculine (el)Feminine (la)
el padre — the fatherla madre — the mother
el hermano — the brotherla hermana — the sister
el hijo — the sonla hija — the daughter
el abuelo — the grandfatherla abuela — the grandmother

For more than one, use los (masculine or mixed) or las (all feminine): los padres = the parents, las hermanas = the sisters.

Handy phrases: mi = my (mi familia, mi padre), tengo = I have (Tengo un hermano), and él es / ella es = he is / she is.

Culture Corner

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 In Spanish-speaking cultures, la familia often means more than parents and kids — grandparents, aunts, uncles, and cousins are part of everyday life, and Sunday meals together are common.

🗣️ People usually say papá and mamá (dad and mom) at home, and padre / madre in more formal talk. Both are correct; this lesson teaches the clear "padre / madre" pair first.

👋 When you meet someone's family, a warm ¡Mucho gusto! (nice to meet you) goes a long way — the same greeting you learned in Week 1.
